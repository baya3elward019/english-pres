01 · The foundation

Let’s make grammar simple.

Today we will present an overview of the English Tense System, specifically focusing on the three foundational simple tenses: Simple Present, Simple Past, and Simple Future. In English grammar, tenses are studied through two core aspects:

Structure (how we build the tense)
and Use (when and why we use it).

Mastering these simple tenses allows us to express daily routines, completed past events, and future decisions clearly and naturally.

Definition: A tense indicates the time of an action or state of being (Past, Present, or Future).

02 · Past Simple

Past Simple Tense

Use it for an action or situation that started and finished in the past, often at a known time.

Sentence structure
Affirmative: Subject + V2
Negative: Subject + didn't + V1
Question: Did + subject + V1…?

Regular verbs often add -ed: walk → walked. Irregular verbs change form: go → went, eat → ate.

Examples
✓ I visited Cairo last year.
✓ He finished work, walked to the beach, and met his friends.
✓ They lived in London when they were young.
✕ She didn't go.   ? Did you see it?
Important: After did or didn't, use the base verb (V1), not V2.
03 · Past Simple

When do we use it?

📍

Completed action

The action is over.

I visited Cairo last year.
➡️

Sequence of actions

Several past events in order.

He finished, walked, and met his friends.
📸

Past habits or states

Something was true in an earlier period.

They lived in London when young.
Common time expressions
YesterdayLast week / month / yearTwo days agoFive years agoIn 2010
Quick clue: “ago”, “yesterday”, and “last…” often point to the Past Simple when the action is finished.
04 · Present Simple

Present Simple Tense

Use it for routines, habits, general facts, and scheduled events.

Sentence structure
He / She / It: verb + s / es / ies
I / You / We / They: V1
Negative: don't / doesn't + V1
Question: Do / Does + subject + V1…?

Examples: study → studies; watch → watches; play → plays.

Examples
✓ She drinks coffee every morning.
✓ The sun rises in the east.
✓ The train leaves at 8:00 PM.
✕ He doesn't like tea.   ? Does she work here?
Watch out: With does or doesn't, the main verb returns to V1: “Does she work?” not “Does she works?”

06 · Future Simple

Future Simple Tense

Use will to talk about future actions, predictions, promises, and offers.

Sentence structure
Affirmative: Subject + will + V1
Negative: Subject + won't + V1
Question: Will + subject + V1…?

“Will” is followed by the base form of the verb for every subject.

Examples
✓ The phone is ringing. I will answer it.
✓ I think it will rain tomorrow.
✓ I will help you with your project.
✕ She won't be late.   ? Will they come?
Contraction: will not = won't. Both express a negative future statement.
07 · Future Simple

What can “will” express?

💡

Instant decision

A decision made at the moment of speaking.

The phone is ringing. I will answer it.
🌦️

Prediction

A belief about the future, often without present evidence.

I think it will rain tomorrow.
🤝

Promise or offer

Offering help or promising to do something.

I will help you with your project.
Common keywords & phrases
Will / Won'tTomorrowNext week / yearSoonIn the futureI think…I hope…Probably

Note: “Will” is not the only way to talk about the future in English, but it is the focus of this lesson.

10 · Final recap

Three tenses. Three patterns.

Past Simple

Subject + V2

Negative: didn't + V1 · Question: Did + subject + V1?

They visited London last year.

Present Simple

Subject + V1 (s/es/ies for he/she/it)

Negative: don't/doesn't + V1 · Question: Do/Does + subject + V1?

She drinks coffee every morning.

Future Simple

Subject + will + V1

Negative: won't + V1 · Question: Will + subject + V1?

I will help you tomorrow.

Before you answer

1. Identify when the action happens.
2. Choose the correct structure.
3. Check the main verb form.
4. Use the context to confirm.
